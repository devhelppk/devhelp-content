Most tutorials teach you to build something that already works. This course teaches you to work on something that is breaking, in a codebase you did not write, with tools that are faster than you.

That is the job in 2026.

## How every lesson works

Each module gives you a working repository and a scenario that breaks it. A ticket like "customers are occasionally charged twice" is the starting point, not a summary at the end. You will:

1. **Trace the data.** Follow one real request from the click to the database row and back.
2. **Form a hypothesis** before you touch anything.
3. **Fix it, then prove the fix** with a test that failed before and passes after.
4. **Write the debrief:** what failed, the pattern behind it, the trade-off you made, and the trigger that should make you look for this next time.

## Foundation mode and industry mode

Lessons are marked with a mode.

- **Foundation**: no AI assistance. You are building the mental model, and there is no shortcut to that.
- **Industry**: AI assistance is expected. You own correctness. The agent's tests prove what the agent thought, not what you need.

Interviews now run in both modes, sometimes in the same hour.

## Try it

Open a project you have written before. Pick one button. Write down, from memory, every file the click passes through until data is saved. Then open the code and check. The gap between the two lists is what this course closes.

## Knowledge check

- Why does the course start from a broken system instead of a blank one?
- What is the difference between foundation mode and industry mode?
- What are the four parts of a debrief?
